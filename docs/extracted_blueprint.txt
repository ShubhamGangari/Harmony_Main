11. Compliance & Claims Governance
Traffic-light rule
GREEN: aroma, fragrance, sensory experience, relaxation ritual, evening routine, refreshing, grounding, uplifting, warming, cooling sensation, personal care, mindful practice, everyday wellness, traditional use (when accurately described).
AMBER: supports relaxation, sleep support, focus, digestion, skin health, stress support, respiratory comfort. Require product-specific substantiation and regulatory review before publication.
RED: cures, treats, prevents disease, heals, immune defence/boost, hormone balancing, cellular repair, reverses ageing, treats anxiety/depression/diabetes/arthritis/asthma, replaces medication, detoxifies/eliminates toxins.
Internal approval workflow
Identify the exact claim.
Ask whether it is objectively measurable.
Identify evidence supporting it.
Check whether evidence applies to the exact product/formulation/use.
Check whether the statement implies diagnosis, treatment or prevention.
Check product classification and applicable regulatory requirements.
Assess ordinary-consumer interpretation.
Approve, rewrite or remove.
Product compliance master sheet
Create an internal record for every product with: Product; Brand; Manufacturer; Importer/distributor where applicable; Product category; Intended use; Approved claims; Prohibited claims; Label/packaging; Batch information; Safety documentation; Regulatory documents; Source/reference for substantiation.

Make the founder visually prominent; trust is a conversion asset.
Use real workshop/experience photography where possible rather than generic wellness stock imagery.

Workshop and participant numbers — only with substantiation.
Professional certifications — list exact certification names and issuing organisations.
Partner/client logos — only with permission.
Workshop photographs and event participation.
Testimonials focused on education, experience and routines rather than disease outcomes.
Research/evidence references where specific scientific statements are made.
Avoid testimonials such as 'cured my insomnia', 'fixed my anxiety', 'treated my arthritis' or equivalent health-outcome claims.
19. Research & Evidence Page
If the brand continues to use 'science' or 'evidence-informed' prominently, build a page explaining the evidence philosophy.
What we know — established botanical, safety and usage information.
What research suggests — emerging or preliminary evidence, clearly labelled.
What we don't know — areas where evidence is limited or inconclusive.
Never convert a single study into a blanket product claim. Clearly distinguish traditional use, laboratory findings, animal studies, observational research, clinical research and established conclusions.
